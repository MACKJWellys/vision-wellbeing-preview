The Therapy · §I — III

Therapy that helps you understand the pattern and change it.

A practical, structured therapy that pays close attention to the loop between what you think, how you feel, what your body does, and what you end up doing about it. The work is to find the parts of that loop that are quietly maintaining the problem, and change them.

§ I — What CBT can help with

What CBT can help with.

Explore some of the areas I work with regularly.

Fig. I

Anxiety & overthinking

Racing thoughts that won’t switch off. “What if” scenarios on loop. Physical tension you can’t quite shake. Anxiety pulls you out of the present and into worry that, when you look at it honestly, is solving nothing.

We map the worry cycle: what triggers it, what keeps it going, and what you do to try to feel safe. From there we work on thought challenging, behavioural experiments, mindfulness, and worry-time techniques.

Typically 6–8 sessions for meaningful shifts. Full course 12–20 sessions.

Fig. II

Perfectionism & high standards

High standards that started as motivation have become the thing in the way. Anything less than perfect feels like failure. Procrastination kicks in when you can’t guarantee a perfect result — then a harsh inner critic kicks in for procrastinating.

We examine the beliefs driving the standards and test whether they’re actually helping you achieve more. Graded exposure to doing things “well enough,” values clarification, behavioural experiments, and self-compassion practice.

Book a free consultation →
Fig. III

Health anxiety

Constant worry about symptoms. Googling conditions late at night. Seeking reassurance from doctors, partners, or family — and the relief never quite lasting. Every physical sensation feels like a possible threat.

We map the worry-checking-relief cycle and work on tolerating uncertainty about health. Examining unhelpful thinking patterns, gradually reducing checking and reassurance behaviours, and learning to live alongside the body’s normal background noise.

Book a free consultation →
Fig. IV

OCD-spectrum difficulties

Intrusive thoughts that feel dangerous or meaningful. Compulsions to check, wash, count, or correct until something feels “just right.” Brief relief from the ritual — and then the cycle repeats with more intensity.

Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) — the gold-standard treatment. We gradually step into triggers while choosing not to perform the compulsion, starting small. Combined with cognitive defusion and uncertainty tolerance.

Book a free consultation →
Fig. V

Performance pressure & exam stress

A presentation, exam, or interview on the horizon, and your mind fills with worst-case scenarios. Heart racing, mind going blank. In the run-up you avoid preparation because thinking about it feels too threatening.

Graded exposure to performance situations, cognitive restructuring to challenge catastrophic thinking, practical relaxation techniques, and confidence-building strategies for the real thing rather than the imagined version.

Book a free consultation →
Fig. VI

Low mood, burnout & motivation

Everything feels heavy and pointless. Activities that used to bring satisfaction now feel like obligations — or you’ve stopped doing them altogether. Burnout looks similar: the drive is gone, and everything feels like too much effort.

Behavioural activation to reintroduce meaningful activities and break the withdrawal cycle, even before motivation returns. Combined with thought monitoring, values work, energy management, and attention to sleep and self-care.

Book a free consultation →
Fig. VII

Insomnia & sleep difficulties

Your mind switches on the moment you get into bed. Going over the day, worrying about tomorrow, watching every minute pass. Tired but wired — and the harder you try to sleep, the more elusive it becomes.

CBT-I (CBT for Insomnia): sleep hygiene, stimulus control, relaxation training, worry-time techniques, and — if appropriate — sleep restriction, a counterintuitive but highly effective technique.

CBT-I is as effective as sleep medication without the side effects, and the improvements tend to last.

Book a free consultation →
Fig. VIII

Panic & physical anxiety symptoms

Heart pounding, breathing tight, a sudden sense that something terrible is about to happen. After the first panic attack, the fear of having another can become all-consuming — leading to avoidance and a shrinking world.

Panic psychoeducation, interoceptive exposure (getting comfortable with the physical sensations), breathing retraining, and gradual exposure to avoided situations. The aim is to lose the fear of panic, which is what allows the panic itself to settle.

§ II — How CBT works

A model for what’s happening, and a process for changing it.

01

We make sense of what’s happening now.

The situations that trigger distress, the thoughts that show up, the feelings in your body, and what you do (or avoid) as a result. We map the pattern in detail.

02

We identify the cycle that’s keeping it going.

Rather than asking only why something started, CBT asks what is maintaining it now. That gives us something concrete to work with from the early sessions on.

03

We test new ways of responding.

Behavioural experiments, exposure work, thought records, problem-solving. Small changes that test the predictions anxiety and low mood quietly make.

04

You build skills you can keep using.

The aim is not to stay in therapy. It’s to leave with a clearer understanding of your patterns, practical tools, and the confidence to use them on your own.

§ III — Inside a session

Sessions are collaborative, focused, and practical.

The first few minutes are usually a check-in: how the week has been, what came up, whether the things we agreed to try last time were possible. From there we set a focus for the session and work through it together.

  1. A clear focus for each session.
  2. Space to talk — but not just talking for its own sake.
  3. Practical tools to take into the week, and review the next time.
  4. A pace that feels manageable, not pushed.
  5. Regular reviews of whether therapy is helping, and adjustments when it isn’t.

You don’t need to know exactly what you want before you start. Part of the early work is getting clear on it together.

You don’t need to have it all figured out.

Sessions are £80 for fifty minutes. The consultation is free, lasts fifteen minutes, and exists so we can both work out whether this is the right fit.
